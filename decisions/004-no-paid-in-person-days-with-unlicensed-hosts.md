# Decision 004: No paid in-person days with unlicensed hosts in the pilot

**Date:** 2026-10-29
**Status:** Active

## Context

The Sprint 4 plan asks travelers for a real commitment, an intro call or a $10 deposit, and
recruits 3 real Shanghai hosts. The China legal check (`discovery/PengPal/legal.md`) found that
under the Tourism Law (Arts. 28, 40, 95, 102), paid guiding requires a guide certificate and
dispatch by a licensed travel agency. "Guiding" includes showing travelers the way, so a host
paid for a half day of help in Shanghai is probably guiding. The host could be fined
¥1,000–10,000, and PengPal could be treated as an unlicensed travel agency. Enforcement against
private companions is rare, but the risk falls mostly on the hosts.

## Options considered

1. **Run paid in-person days with unlicensed hosts anyway**: This is the model the prototype
   shows, and it tests real willingness to pay most directly. Cons: it's illegal as written, and
   the hosts, who are students and young locals, carry the fines.
2. **Only licensed guides, or hosts dispatched by a licensed agency**: The legally clean option.
   Cons: it takes time to find an agency partner and agree a revenue split, and licensed guides
   may not fit the "young local friend" positioning. Not doable by Nov 9.
3. **Keep the pilot's paid commitments outside China**: Ask for refundable $10 deposits and free
   or paid pre-trip intro calls, with no paid on-the-ground days yet. Cons: a deposit is a
   weaker signal than a paid day, and paid in-person days stay untested for now.

## Decision

Option 3 for Sprint 4. Option 2 becomes the path to paid in-person days. The deciding reason:
we won't put legal risk on hosts to get a stronger test signal. A refundable deposit still
measures whether travelers commit money, which is the sprint's question.

In practice:
- The prototype can keep showing priced half and full days, because nothing is sold there.
- The commitment asked of travelers is a refundable $10 deposit or an intro call.
- Host recruiting asks each candidate whether they already hold a guide certificate.

## What would change our mind

- A Chinese lawyer or the Shanghai Culture and Tourism Bureau says daily-life help without
  sightseeing commentary isn't guiding under Art. 2.
- A licensed Shanghai agency agrees to dispatch PengPal hosts.
- Enough recruited hosts already hold guide certificates to run paid days through option 2.
