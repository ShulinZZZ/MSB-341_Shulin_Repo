## Questions and responses

**Q1. What are the most common MCAT preparation challenges students bring to you?**

Most students struggle with time management and knowing what to study. They have to balance MCAT preparation with classes, research, volunteering, and sometimes part-time jobs. Another common problem is remembering material they learned in earlier science courses. Many students spend a lot of time studying but aren't sure whether they're making progress.

**Q2. How do you currently help students develop study plans?**

I usually start by asking about their target score, test date, current academic schedule, and previous practice exam results. Then I help them break their preparation into smaller weekly goals. I encourage students to focus more on their weaker subjects while regularly reviewing material they've already studied.

**Q3. What makes an MCAT study schedule realistic and effective?**

A good study schedule needs to be flexible and fit the student's actual availability. Students shouldn't plan to study five hours every day if they have a full course load. I also think an effective schedule should include practice questions, regular review, full-length exams, and time to analyze mistakes. It's important to adjust the schedule based on progress.

**Q4. What is your impression of Gapmap's personalized study planning approach?**

I think it's a useful concept because many students don't know how to create an effective study plan on their own. I like that Gapmap identifies weaker subjects and recommends what to study each week. However, I would want the schedule to adjust when students fall behind or improve in certain areas. That would make the personalization more meaningful.

**Q5. How useful would the mastery map be for identifying student weaknesses?**

I think the mastery map could be very helpful because it gives students a visual overview of their strengths and weaknesses. Instead of looking at a long list of subjects, students can quickly see where they need improvement. However, the results need to be based on enough practice questions to be reliable.

**Q6. What concerns do you have about the eight-question diagnostic?**

My biggest concern is that eight questions aren't enough to accurately measure a student's understanding of all the MCAT subjects. A student might answer one question correctly but still struggle with that topic overall. I think the short diagnostic is fine as a starting point, but Gapmap should make it clear that the results are preliminary and update them as students complete more questions.

**Q7. How should Gapmap support students who miss planned study sessions?**

I think Gapmap should automatically adjust the study schedule when students miss a few days or a week. This happens frequently, especially during midterms and finals. Instead of making students feel like they're falling further behind, the app should help them reorganize their remaining study time and prioritize the most important topics. I would also let students choose how many hours they can realistically study each week.

**Q8. What information would you want to see in a student's progress report?**

I would want to see how much of their study plan they've completed, which subjects they're improving in, and which topics still need attention. Practice question accuracy and full-length practice exam scores would also be helpful. I think it's important to show actual improvement rather than just the number of hours spent studying.

**Q9. Would you recommend Gapmap to students in its current form? Why or why not?**

I would recommend it as a tool to help students get started and organize their MCAT preparation, but I wouldn't recommend relying on it as their only study resource yet. The personalized plan and mastery map are promising, but the app still needs better progress tracking and more accurate assessments. Students need to know that the recommendations are based on their actual performance.

**Q10. What improvements would you prioritize before recommending it?**

My first priority would be making the study plan flexible so it adjusts when students miss sessions. Second, I would add a way to track completed activities and practice question results. Third, I would improve the diagnostic assessment so the mastery map becomes more accurate over time. I also think students should be able to customize their weekly study hours. Those improvements would make Gapmap much more practical for everyday MCAT preparation.
