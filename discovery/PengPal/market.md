# Part 1: Who PengPal is for

## Bottom line

**The best early adopters are experienced independent travelers on their first mainland China
trip.** They have already researched it, they have paid for local help elsewhere, and they still
worry about getting through daily life. Three sessions fit this best: T04, T06 and T07. They had
dated trips 4–7 months out, had paid $35–150 for local help before, offered $40–100, and all
three joined the waitlist and referred someone.

What they're buying is **daily life made easy and someone local they can trust**, not
sightseeing. Language and getting around came up far more than payments did, even though
payments was the problem we assumed going in.

## Early adopters

An early adopter has the problem, knows it, has already tried to solve it, and has a trip coming
up. I scored each session on those signals:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| **T06** | Apr 2027 | Family attractions, getting around | $150 family tour | $80–100 | yes | yes | **Yes** |
| **T07** | May 2027 | Forums, non-Google map apps | $35 walking tour | $40–50 | yes | yes | **Yes** |
| T01 | not recorded | AI-made plan | ~US minimum wage/hr | $50/day | yes | yes | Likely (timing unknown) |

| T05 | Feb 2027 (semester) | Exchange groups | never | $20 | no | no | No |
| T03\* | ~2 years | Relies on local friends | never | "more" | yes | yes | Not yet (timing) |
| T08\* | Nov 2026 | Concierge, translation apps | $120/day | $100+ | yes | no | Out of scope |

**What the three early adopters share:**
- **Experienced travelers, new to China:** they've been to Japan, Korea, Southeast Asia or Europe,
  and this is their first mainland trip.
- **They did their homework and still worry.** Research didn't remove the fear of daily life on
  the ground.
- **They already pay for local help:** $35–150 for walking tours or private tours elsewhere.
- **Trust decides whether they book.** All three asked for verification and reviews. T06 and
  T07 called the video intro essential. T07 wants a female host, and T06 wants reviews from
  other families.
- **They want the all-in price upfront** (T04, T06, T07).
- **Their trips are 4–7 months out, not 1–3.** This differs from the team doc's persona, which
  targets trips within 1–3 months. Nobody in T01–T10 had a trip that soon except T08, who is out
  of scope. Either recruit people with sooner trips, or accept that early adopters commit months
  ahead. The second fits a refundable deposit toward a trip pass.

All three are women, and the sample came through our own networks. Watch whether this holds in
T11–T18 rather than treating it as part of the target.

**What this means for the trip pass:**
- **Setup help can't be only about Alipay.** The pass should lead with "you'll be understood and
  you'll get around." The local friend is the answer to communicating, the most common pain, and
  the pre-trip checklist covers transit and payments. T11–T18 asks which part matters most, which
  tests this.
- **Trust features are part of the product, not extras:**
  - a verified host
  - reviews
  - an intro call before committing
  - meeting in public places
  - an emergency contact

## Personas

These three personas cover the strongest segments. They're fictional composites, with no real
names.

### 1. Maya, the independent first-timer (primary). From T04, T07, T01

- Late 20s to 30s. Has traveled in Japan, Korea or Southeast Asia, going solo or with one friend.
- First mainland China trip, 4–7 months out, Shanghai plus one or two other cities.
- Has watched YouTube and looked into Alipay and the metro, and still feels uneasy: *"I need
  someone to make China feel less intimidating."* (T04)
- Doesn't want a guide for the whole trip. She wants a trusted local friend at the start: *"I'd
  probably book someone for my first day, just to feel comfortable navigating the city
  afterward."* (T07)
- Will pay **$40–50** for a half day. She expects to see the all-in price upfront.
- Books when the host is verified, reviewed, speaks good English and understands her culture.
  She may want a female host.

### 2. Claire, the family planner (secondary). From T06, T03\*

- A parent traveling with a partner and two kids. An experienced family traveler in Europe and
  Japan.
- First China trip, 5 months out, with several cities.
- *"Traveling with children in a country where we don't speak the language feels stressful. I want
  someone reliable who can help us."* (T06)
- Pays for ease, not expertise: *"I would pay more for someone who makes the trip easier, not
  necessarily someone who knows more history."* Offered **$80–100** for a day, and has paid $150.
- Needs a host who knows kid-friendly places, a clear cancellation policy, background checks and
  reviews from other families.
- **Under decision 005:** she wants dependable help, and an unpaid volunteer is harder to sell her.
  The pre-trip call and setup help may suit her better than the local friend.

### 3. Peter, the patient explorer (later). From T10

- A retiree traveling with a spouse. Plans flights and hotels alone, and has used private guides
  in Vietnam.
- First China trip, about 10 months out.
- *"I can organize flights and hotels, but navigating an unfamiliar country without speaking the
  language is what concerns me."*
- Pays **$80** for someone patient and clear. He struggled with the app itself and needs larger
  text and a human on the phone.

**Not our customer now:**
- **Budget students (T02, T05):** "more like a friendship app than a tour guide app"
- **Business travelers (T08):** "I'm not paying for a tour. I'm paying to save time and avoid
  expensive mistakes."

## Ideal customer profile

**PengPal's ideal customer right now:**

| | Profile |
|---|---|
| **Who** | An English-speaking traveler from the US, UK, Canada, Australia or Western Europe; solo, a couple or with one friend |
| **Trip** | First trip to mainland China, dated within the next 3–8 months, with **Shanghai** on the route |
| **Travel history** | Has traveled independently in Asia or Europe, and has paid **$35–150** for a walking tour, food tour or private guide there |
| **Situation** | Has already researched China (YouTube, forums, Alipay) and still worries about communicating, getting around and paying |
| **Wants** | A trusted local friend and day-one confidence, not attractions or a guide for the whole trip |
| **Will pay** | About $40–50 for a half day of help (Sprint 3). The trip pass price is untested |
| **Decides on** | Verification, reviews, an intro call before committing, a clear all-in price, and a host who understands their culture |
| **Signals to look for** | Has booked or dated the trip, joined the waitlist, refers friends |

**Not the ideal customer:**
- repeat visitors and Mandarin speakers
- students on a budget or staying for a semester or more
- business travelers who need an interpreter
- people who prefer packaged group tours

**Where the ICP differs from the team doc (`target-customers.md`):**
- **Trip timing:** the doc targets trips 1–3 months out, and the data supports 3–8 months.
- **Age:** the doc says 22–35, but only T04's age (26) was recorded, so age is unverified.
- **Worries:** the doc leads with payments and VPNs, and the data leads with communicating and
  getting around.

Both agree on the core: a Western first-timer who wants a local friend who understands their
culture.

**How T11–T18 test this:**
- Do people matching the ICP commit (deposit or paid call) more often than the others?
- Which part of the trip pass matters most to them?
- Does the early-adopter pattern hold with travelers from outside our own networks?
