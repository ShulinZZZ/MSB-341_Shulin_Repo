# PengPal traveler interview guide (Sprint 3)

**Goal:** Find out whether first-time China travelers would pay for a local host, at what
price, and what in the prototype confuses them (see `sprints/sprint-3-plan.md`).
**Length:** ~30 min. Video call with screen share, or in person on their phone.
**Prototype:** https://shulinzzz.github.io/MSB-341_Shulin_Repo/product/pengpal/
**Notes:** one file per session from `T00-notes-template.md` → `T01.md` … `T10.md`. No real
names, emails or phone numbers. Use "T03, US, planning Shanghai in spring".

Ground rules for me:
- Ask about what they **have done**, not what they **would** do. Past behavior beats predictions.
- During the walkthrough, **don't help or explain**. If they get stuck, ask "What would you
  expect to happen?" and wait. Note where they got stuck.
- Write down their exact words for the pay question and anything surprising.

---

## 0. Screener (before booking the call)

Only interview people who say yes to both:
1. Are you planning a trip to mainland China in the next 12 months?
2. Would it be your first time in mainland China?

Also note: Which cities? Traveling solo, with a partner, or with a group? Where are you from?

## 1. Intro (2 min)

> "Thanks for doing this. I'm testing an early idea for people visiting China for the first
> time. There are no right answers. I didn't design this to be perfect, so honest criticism
> helps me most. Is it OK if I take notes? I won't record your name."

## 2. Their trip and their worries (8 min) — *before showing anything*

1. Tell me about the trip you're planning. Where, when, who with, and why China?
2. What have you done so far to prepare? (Visa, apps, bookings, videos, asking friends?)
3. What part of the trip are you least sure how to handle?
   - Only if they don't bring it up themselves, ask about each: paying for things, getting
     around, ordering food, language, safety, internet/VPN.
4. Think about the last time you traveled somewhere you didn't speak the language. What was
   hardest, and what did you do about it?
5. Have you ever paid for a guide, tour, or local experience (Airbnb Experiences,
   GetYourGuide, a private guide)? What did you pay, and was it worth it?
6. If you had a problem in China tomorrow, like your card not working at a restaurant, what
   would you do?

*Listen for:* how worried they actually are, what they've already tried, and whether they
already spend money on guides. If they aren't worried at all, note that. It's evidence too.

## 3. Prototype walkthrough (12 min)

> "Here's a prototype. It's fake: no real hosts, nothing gets charged. Please think out loud
> as you go: what you're looking at, what you expect, what's confusing."

Give one task at a time. Note what they say and where they hesitate.

| # | Task to read aloud | Screens | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Set it up for your trip." | Welcome / onboarding | Do they get the pitch from the first screen? |
| 2 | "Pick someone you'd want to spend a day with." | Host list → profile | What they look at to decide: badges, reviews, languages? Do they trust the hosts? |
| — | **Before they open a profile**, ask: "What would you expect a half day with one of these people to cost?" | Host list | Their number *before* they see the $24 / $40 / $80 prices |
| 3 | "Set up a call with them before you commit." | Video intro | Does the time-zone display make sense? Would they actually do the call? |
| 4 | "Book a day with them." | Book and pay | Which length they pick (2-hr visit $24, half day $40, full day $80) and their reaction to the 15% fee as its own line |
| 5 | "Get ready for the trip." | Prep checklist | Which items are new to them? Is this useful on its own? |
| 6 | "It's the day of the trip. Find where to meet, and what you'd do if something went wrong." | Day of trip | Do they find the safety button? Does it make them feel safer? |
| 7 | "Finish the trip." | Review | Any friction. Skip if short on time. |

After the walkthrough:
1. What was the most useful part? What was the least useful part?
2. Was anything confusing or missing?
3. What would stop you from booking this?
4. How would you feel about spending a day with a stranger you met through an app? What
   would make you trust them?

## 4. The pay question (5 min) — *ask this the same way every time*

> **"Would you pay for this, and how much?"**

Write their answer word for word. Then:
1. (If yes) "For a 2-hour visit, a half day or a full day? Just you, or split with your group?"
2. "The prototype showed $24 for a 2-hour visit, $40 for a half day and $80 for a full day,
   plus a 15% fee. Is that too cheap, about right, getting expensive, or too expensive?"
3. "At what price would you stop considering it?"
4. "What would you do instead if this didn't exist?"

*Polite "yes" is not a yes.* A strong signal is a specific price **and** a real next step
(waitlist, introducing a friend, asking when it launches).

## 5. Commitment and wrap-up (3 min)

1. "We're hand-matching the first trips in Chongqing and Shanghai. Want me to add you to the
   waitlist?" Record yes/no and which city. Keep any contact info **out of the repo**.
2. "Do you know anyone else planning a first trip to China I could talk to?"
3. "Anything I should have asked but didn't?"

Thank them.

---

## After each session (10 min, same day)

Fill in `T0X.md` from the template while it's fresh. Log the pay answer, the price number,
and the waitlist answer in the session file's summary table, so the findings write-up can add
up all 10 sessions quickly.
