## What it means for PengPal

Both concerns match gaps already open in our plan. The competitor positioning table was moved to
Sprint 5, and the Rollins judges also asked "how is this different from a travel agent or paid
guide?"

**1. Existing alternatives (first pass)**

| How first-timers handle it today | What they get | Where it falls short | PengPal's angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Go alone + translation apps, blogs, YouTube | Free, flexible | Payment and app setup fails, menus and maps are confusing, they miss local places | A friend who sets up the apps on Day One and shows where locals eat |
| Paid private guide (e.g. ToursByLocals) | Expert sightseeing | Built around attractions; a professional, not a friend; expensive | Ordinary local life, with a host their age |
| Group tours / travel agents | Everything handled | Rigid, crowded, not "real" China | A personal, flexible day |
| A friend who lives in China | Trust, local life | Most first-timers don't have one | "Travel like you have a friend there" |
| Trip.com, Klook and similar apps | English support, bookable tickets and tours | A help line and listings, not a person beside you | A real person for day-one setup and local life |
| Local-friend marketplaces (Withlocals, Showaround) | Book a local to show you around | Mainland China coverage unclear (to check); framed as paid guiding | Hosts who lived in your country, plus China app and payment setup |
| Language-exchange apps (HelloTalk, Tandem) | Free practice partners, sometimes meetups | Unvetted strangers; not built around a first trip | Vetted partners matched to your trip and home country. The closest alternative if we launch as a language exchange |

**Candidate unique advantage:** hosts who studied or lived in the traveler's own country, so they
understand both cultures. Plus our own WeChat networks in Shanghai for host supply. This still
needs testing: do travelers pick a host *because* of that match?

**2. Monetization (what we assume today)**

- Three models are in play, and we have to pick one: the Oct 22 pitch (host keeps 80%, PengPal
  20%, about $24 on a ~$120 full day); the prototype ($80 full day + 15% fee = $92 all in); and
  decision 005, proposed (unpaid hosts, the traveler pays PengPal a matching fee). Decision 005 is
  the one most likely to hold under China's guide rules
- This week's test: a $10 refundable deposit. Fallback offer: a paid pre-trip video call with a
  local (~$20)
- Still open: the real price point, how hosts are paid across borders and taxed (answered in the
  Sprint 4 legal check: a lump sum after the trip, and the host declares it as income; not needed
  if hosts are unpaid), and whether deposits turn into paid bookings
