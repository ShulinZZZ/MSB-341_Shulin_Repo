## Your retro

*The builder's own answers, recorded word for word.*

**Did you hit the goal?** Yes, I have not only hit the goal but have a more clear direction for my next sprint based on what I have done in this sprint. During Sprint 2, I have conducted the advisor interview and 10 prototype tests and summarized the results into two docs that I just downloaded to the MSB folder. I have also updated the prototype which was the one I used for interviews. I have finished all five goals listed on the sprint 2 plan.

**What did the report show you?** What surprised me most was that different students could interpret the same Gapmap features very differently. For example, I designed the knowledge tree to motivate students by showing which concepts they need to review, but some students might feel discouraged when they see too many red leaves, especially freshmen who haven't taken all the prerequisite courses yet. I also hadn't considered that students might mistake marking a concept as "reviewed" for actually mastering it. Another unexpected finding was that students close to their MCAT test date may not trust the projected score because it increases by about 1.15 points every week, even if they haven't completed their study plan. I originally thought the score chart would motivate students, but it could actually create unrealistic expectations. Finally, I realized that students who already use spreadsheets to organize their studying might find Gapmap less useful if they cannot edit their weekly schedules or mark tasks as completed. These findings showed me that features I considered helpful could actually cause confusion or frustration depending on a student's experience and preparation stage.

**If the plan changed, why?** My plan changed because the interview feedback made me realize that Gapmap would require more development and resources than I originally expected. Students need more than personalized study plans; they also need accurate assessments, flexible scheduling, and progress tracking based on actual performance. Building these features would take significant time, and Gapmap would have to compete with established MCAT preparation platforms. Because of these challenges, I'm considering switching to a new business idea called **PengPal**, an app that connects first-time foreign travelers in China with young, English-speaking Chinese locals who help them navigate transportation, payments, food, and authentic local experiences. The idea came from my experience helping BYU friends travel in China when they struggled with Chinese apps, QR-code payments, and transportation. Unlike Gapmap, PengPal could be tested with a small group of travelers and local hosts through manually arranged bookings before investing heavily in software development. I'm still evaluating both ideas, but the research helped me recognize the importance of choosing a business that solves a clear problem and can be validated quickly.

PengPal is easier to validate because I can test whether people actually want the service without building a fully functional app. I could start by recruiting a few English-speaking Chinese locals and connecting them manually with foreign travelers visiting China. By arranging a few real trips, I could quickly find out whether travelers are willing to pay, how much they would pay, and whether they are satisfied enough to recommend the service to others. In comparison, Gapmap would require a more advanced product with accurate MCAT assessments, adaptive study schedules, and progress tracking before I could fully test its value. It would also take months to determine whether Gapmap actually helps students improve their MCAT scores. With PengPal, I could get meaningful feedback immediately after each trip and measure real bookings instead of just asking people whether they like the idea. Although PengPal would still require careful attention to host verification, traveler safety, and local regulations, I think its core business concept would be faster and less expensive to test.

**Actual difficulty:** Actual Difficulty: 4/5

The process was more difficult than I originally expected. I thought building Gapmap's prototype and getting feedback from pre-med students would be relatively straightforward. However, the interviews revealed that creating a truly useful MCAT study tool requires much more than a personalized study plan. Students need accurate assessments, flexible scheduling, and reliable progress tracking, which would take significant time and technical resources to develop. I also didn't expect to reconsider my entire business idea during this process. Initially, I thought I would just improve Gapmap based on user feedback, but I started questioning whether it was the best opportunity to pursue. This led me to explore PengPal, which I believe could be easier to validate through real bookings without building a complete app. The biggest unexpected difficulty was realizing that I needed to evaluate not just how to improve my prototype, but whether I should continue with the business idea at all.

**What will you change next sprint?** Next sprint, I will create a clickable prototype for PengPal that shows how travelers can find a local host, schedule an introductory video call, and book a trip. I want to use this prototype to interview potential travelers and test whether they would actually pay for the service before investing more time in development.
